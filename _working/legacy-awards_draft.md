## Context

**From Projects to People**

For 17 years, The Sprout Fund primarily supported specific projects and organizations. However, as the organization prepared to sunset in 2018, leadership recognized that "projects are only as good as the people behind them". To conclude its operations with a forward-looking investment, Sprout designed a final round of grantmaking that functioned as a "Sprout-sized version of a MacArthur genius award".

The objective was to identify "Trustees of Sprout's Legacy"—champions entrusted to carry forward Sprout’s vision and enduring values through their ongoing work in Pittsburgh. While early internal scenarios considered funding 10 projects for 10 years, the final strategy evolved to support a broader cohort of 50 individual change agents. This decision validated the thesis that investing in "quirky, scrappy, and authentically Pittsburgh" people yields profound long-term returns for the community.

## Highlights

### The Call to Action: "Who Embodies Sprout?"

In April 2018, Sprout issued a public call to action via social media and email, asking the community to "help us find the Pittsburghers who will commit to carrying our vision forward". The campaign encouraged the public to nominate individuals by tagging them on social media with the hashtag #SproutSunset or submitting via email, specifically citing why the nominee embodied Sprout’s ideals.

### The Seven Sprout Ideals

The selection process was anchored in seven distinct characteristics. Nominees were evaluated based on their alignment with these "Sprout Ideals," and finalists were asked to self-identify with the characteristic that best described their work.

**Distribution of Ideals within the 2018 Cohort:**

1.  **Driven by deep personal passion and sense of purpose (36%)**
    * *The dominant characteristic of the cohort, reflecting a group highly motivated by intrinsic mission.*
2.  **Challengers of the status quo and unrelenting in their pursuits (22%)**
    * *The second most common trait, highlighting the cohort's disruptive potential.*
3.  **Creative doers with uncanny insights and avant-garde approaches (16%)**
4.  **Serious about their work but not always about themselves (8%)**
5.  **Audacious thinkers with small starts and big plans (8%)**
6.  **Civic-minded, tireless pioneers always with their eye on what’s needed next (6%)**
7.  **Quirky, scrappy, and authentically Pittsburgh (4%)**
    * *Interestingly, while this phrase was often used to describe Sprout itself, very few individual leaders chose it as their primary identifier.*

### The Selection Process

The process moved rapidly from nomination to selection over an eight-week period in Spring 2018.
1.  **Nomination:** The community submitted over 500 nominations for 300 individuals.
2.  **Finalist Notification:** Selected finalists received a unique "nominee code" and were invited to complete a brief application.
3.  **Application:** Finalists submitted a personal statement (limited to 1,000 characters) describing how they would inspire others and carry Sprout's ideals forward.
4.  **Final Decision:** A decision-making committee comprised of staff, board members, and key alumni reviewed materials and selected the final 50 awardees.

**Financial Disposition:**
Finalists were given the option to receive the $1,000 award as taxable individual income or to direct it to a nonprofit of their choice. The decision was nearly split down the middle, with **52% choosing to donate** the funds to organizations such as *Hip-Hop On L.O.C.K.*, *Remembering Hiroshima*, and *Grow Pittsburgh*, while **48% accepted the funds** to support their personal practice or livelihood.

### Immediate Results (2018)

The awards were announced publicly on June 14, 2018. The cohort was celebrated at the "Sprout Sunset Party" on the summer solstice, June 21, 2018. The immediate result was a powerful affirmation of the region's human capital. The group included artists, activists, nonprofit founders, and technologists, creating a diverse cross-section of Pittsburgh's civic leadership.

### Long-Term Impact (2025 Assessment)

A definitive verification report conducted in 2025 analyzed the trajectories of the 50 awardees seven years post-award. The findings confirmed that the selection criteria were highly predictive of future leadership impact. The cohort largely followed three distinct maturation paths:

* **🚀 The "Founder-to-Scaler" Path:** Awardees who professionalized and scaled their 2018-era "small starts" into national organizations. Examples include **Kelauni Jasmyn** (Black Tech Nation), **Leah Lizarondo** (412 Food Rescue), and **Sloane Davidson** (Hello Neighbor).
* **🌳 The "Civic Bedrock" Path:** Awardees who doubled down on their local roles, becoming essential stabilizers in their communities. Examples include **Lisa Freeman** (Freeman Family Farm) and **Terri L. Baltimore** (Hill District historian).
* **🏗️ The "Ecosystem Builder" Path:** Awardees who transitioned into infrastructure building to support other creators. Examples include **Ryan Lammie** (Radiant Hall -> SSR Creative) and **Anne Marie Toccket** (Posner Foundation).

By 2025, the "scrappy" outsiders of 2018 had effectively become the new institutional insiders and leaders of the region.

## Open Questions for the Archive

The following questions remain unanswered by the current dataset and could be addressed with further archival research:

1.  **Nomination Network:** Who were the primary sources of the 500+ nominations? Was there a correlation between specific nominators (e.g., previous Sprout grantees vs. general public) and successful awardees?
2.  **The "Non-Selected" Cohort:** The current archive only contains data on the 50 winners. Is there a record of the ~250 other nominees or the finalists who were not selected? Tracking their trajectories would provide a valuable control group to measure the specific impact of the "Legacy Awardee" designation.
