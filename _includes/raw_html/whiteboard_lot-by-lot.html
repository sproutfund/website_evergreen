Lot by Lot was envisioned as a catalyst for helping groups and organizations reclaim vacant lots for more productive community use.

Proposed to align with the City’s neighborhood revitalization efforts, this program aimed to enable vacant property holders to support creative and productive reuses for their lands. It sought to harness stakeholder support, grassroots community enthusiasm, and professional expertise to improve the vacant lot situation in Pittsburgh.

The program would have allowed citizens and communities to access vacant lots and determine their best uses. It intended to connect them with the necessary tools and resources to transform these lots into community gardens, parks, public art spaces, recreation areas, and more. Repurposing could have been both temporary and long-term, depending on the community's plans for the land.

While the reclamation of vacant lots would have incurred costs, it promised to significantly impact communities and reshape the city’s attitude toward vacant properties. The Sprout Fund proposed a “community capital campaign” to invest in neighborhood futures, aiming to create resources for transforming lots and building momentum for a comprehensive reclamation program.
